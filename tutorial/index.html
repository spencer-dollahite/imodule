"+" = this or more, without starving the host OS. The guest ships at 8 GB RAM / 60 GB virtual disk (it grows as labs run). On Intel/AMD PCs and Intel Macs, enable CPU virtualization (VT-x / AMD-V) in BIOS/UEFI; Apple-Silicon Macs need no such setting.
These numbers are a general baseline, not a one-size-fits-all guarantee — they won't be ideal for every hardware/hypervisor combination. Feel free to adjust the VM's vCPU, RAM and disk, and other hypervisor settings (display/graphics especially, plus clipboard and networking) to suit your machine and smooth out performance. There are too many combinations to list, so it's worth experimenting and doing a little of your own research to settle on a good setup.

Pick the native path. Run the appliance that matches your machine's CPU, virtualized natively (an Intel Mac runs the x86_64 appliance natively, not emulated). Emulation (a non-native guest, e.g. the x86_64 appliance on an Apple-Silicon Mac) is slow and a last resort, only if the native path won't work. On Apple Silicon, VirtualBox is recommended. VirtualBox and UTM need no account; VMware is free but needs a Broadcom account.
